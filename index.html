WildSteps case study
UI/UX · Mobile App · Android

WildSteps — Camping & Hiking App

A comprehensive mobile experience for camping and hiking in Sri Lanka, bringing location discovery, gear rentals, safety tools and community features into one place.

TimelineFeb–Oct 2025
Team3 members
Design toolsFigma · Canva
MethodAgile · 3 sprints

At a glance

Our three-person team completed the project over roughly eight months. My design focus included understanding the needs of outdoor enthusiasts, exploring user journeys, prioritising key features and shaping the interface through research, sketches and prototyping.

Problem

Camping and hiking information in Sri Lanka is fragmented across multiple sources. Users have to piece together locations, gear rentals, safety guidance and weather updates, making trip planning inefficient and confusing.

The lack of structured guidance especially affects beginners and reduces overall engagement in outdoor activities.

Solution

WildSteps centralises core camping and hiking needs in one mobile application: safe location discovery, rental gear, trip planning, real-time weather and safety updates, emergency support and community reviews.

The integrated platform simplifies trip planning, improves safety and creates a more connected outdoor experience.

Process

We followed an iterative design process so each step—from research to prototyping—contributed directly to a more informed and user-centred solution.

User research

We reviewed existing camping and hiking apps to identify gaps in current features and user experience. Those findings helped us define a more integrated solution tailored to Sri Lankan outdoor enthusiasts.

The empathy map helped surface user behaviours, needs and pain points, while the MoSCoW technique helped prioritise the features that mattered most to the user experience.

Sketches

Early sketches explored multiple ways of organising key tasks before committing to polished UI. This made it easier to compare layouts quickly and keep the team aligned around the core flows.

Prototyping

We then moved the approved flows into an interactive prototype, refining hierarchy, navigation and interaction patterns before finalising the experience.

Outcome

The final concept brings location discovery, trip planning, safety support, rental management and community functionality into one Android experience. The project strengthened my ability to translate user needs into prioritised features and clear interface experiences within a collaborative team.

What I learned

This project showed me how much stronger a product becomes when research, prioritisation and design decisions stay connected. I also developed my confidence in user-centred thinking, prototyping, collaborative decision-making and translating user needs into practical features.

Veloura case study
UI/UX · Website · Solo

Veloura — Skincare & Beauty Website

An e-commerce experience for skincare, haircare and cosmetics, designed around clear product discovery, responsive browsing and a structured shopping flow.

FocusUX/UI Design
Design workWireframes · UI mockups
ExperienceResponsive shopping journey
ProjectIndividual work

Business scenario

Veloura operates as an online marketplace offering skincare, haircare and cosmetic products. The experience needed to support customers moving from discovery to comparison and purchase with as little friction as possible.

Business needs

  • Mobile, tablet and desktop-friendly navigation.
  • Adaptable database structure for inventory and order management.
  • High visual clarity for browsing and product information.
  • Personalised discounts and product suggestions for loyal customers.

Core offering

The platform brings together skincare, haircare, makeup, conditioners, gifts and related beauty products with clear product categorisation and search.

Key features

Functional

Shopping cart and checkout, order placement and confirmation, product browsing/search, categorised browsing and filters.

Non-functional

Usable navigation, quick page performance, responsive layouts and a consistent cross-device experience.

Design process

1. Structure & wireframing

I began by organising the main shopping sections and mapping how users would move between product discovery, product details, the cart and checkout.

2. Interface design

I explored page layouts and UI mockups with a focus on clear product categories, readable product information, consistent components and a calm visual hierarchy.

3. Responsive experience

I considered how navigation, product cards and key shopping actions should adapt across desktop, tablet and mobile screens.

What I learned

This project strengthened my understanding of e-commerce UX, especially how information architecture, product categorisation, visual hierarchy and a clear shopping journey work together.

Smart Buy case study
Website · Solo Project

Smart Buy Grocery

A grocery shopping experience planned around straightforward product discovery, clear categories, accessible product information and a familiar purchase journey.

FocusUX/UI Design
ExperienceGrocery e-commerce
TypeIndividual project

Business scenario

Smart Buy Grocery is an online grocery store where users can browse and purchase everyday products such as fruits, vegetables, dairy and household essentials. The experience was designed to keep browsing simple while working well across phones, tablets and desktop screens.

Business needs

  • Make product categories easy to scan and understand.
  • Keep product cards and product information clear and consistent.
  • Create simple navigation between browsing, cart and checkout.
  • Support a comfortable shopping experience across screen sizes.

What I learned

  • Practised organising product information into clear, scannable layouts.
  • Explored visual hierarchy, spacing and consistency across shopping components.
  • Considered how key interactions should behave across the shopping journey.
  • Learned how responsive decisions influence usability on different screen sizes.

Challenges

The main challenges were creating a cohesive visual theme, keeping layouts responsive at different widths and maintaining consistency as the experience grew.

How I improved them

I refined the colour system, simplified spacing rules, reviewed layouts at different screen sizes and kept iterating through small usability improvements.

Design process

Information architecture

I organised the main product categories and considered how shoppers could move through the store without unnecessary steps.

Wireframing & layout

I explored the placement of navigation, product grids, product information and shopping actions to create a familiar and easy-to-follow experience.

Iteration

I refined the hierarchy, spacing and responsive layouts to keep the interface consistent and comfortable to use across devices.
